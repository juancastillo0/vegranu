Nutritional guidelines are important since they provide a condensed representation of the data with multiple considerations, such as different levels of the evidence hierarchy and the consensus of local and international institutions. In some cases, they also include externalities which affect the health of the whole population such as the food's environmental impact, biological and chemical pollution, antibiotic use and increase risk of epidemics. 

Sometimes they over-index on micronutrient adequacy by using foods such as dairy without considering alternatives, like plant-based milks/yogurts or other accessible foods high in similar micronutrients (vegetables, legumes, nuts and seeds). Furthermore, some do not consider that most of the world population is [lactose intolerant](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/lactose-intolerance/definition-facts#common). Another consideration are the differences in recommended intake for some micronutrients. In particular, for calcium the World Health Organization (WHO) recommends [500 mg/day vs 1000 mg/day in the US](https://www.health.harvard.edu/press_releases/expert-says-guidelines-recommend-too-much-calcium). Other times, they are too specific to their country in terms of cultural choices, and the opportunities for improvement in the population may not apply to you (distinct rates of obesity, cardiovascular disease or specific cancers per country). Some might be influenced by the food industry or other countries recommendations. For example, the recommendations to reduce a specific nutrients (sugar, cholesterol, saturated fats) that fail to include the main food sources, so the reader understands which specific foods they should limit. The [2026 Dietary Guidelines for Americans](https://www.cacfp.org/assets/pdf/Dietary+Guidelines+for+Americans%2C+2025%E2%80%932030/) explicitly states: "We are realigning our food system to support American farmers, ranchers, and companies who grow and produce real food", which may not result in the optimization of the health of the population.
